# ADR-0060: The estate map is generated

- Status: Proposed
- Date: 2026-09-19
- Related: ADR-0016 (submodule composition, amended 2026-09-07 for depth
  three), ADR-0020 (one document per subject; clause 5, the manifest is the
  estate and no document lists repositories), ADR-0011 (naming: the path is
  the name), ADR-0010 (a technology is its own repository), ADR-0058
  (Foundation is a noun; Platform is a lifecycle, and its amendment unmounted
  three modules)

## In brief

- Theme: The shape of the estate
- Subject: How a reader sees the whole estate, and why it is not drawn by hand
- Name: The estate map is generated
- Order: 14
- Concepts: estate map; declared repository; mounted repository; declared maturity

**The whole estate is one generated document. `New-XmipEstateMap` reads
`architecture.toml` and the `.gitmodules` files and writes
`doc/architecture/estate-map.md`, which names every declared repository, the
domain and parent it sits under, its declared maturity, and whether it is
composed in this working tree. `test/EstateMap.Test.ps1` regenerates it and
fails when the committed file differs, so it cannot be edited by hand and
cannot rot. The hand-drawn tree in `repository-model.md` section 7 stays: it
says where modules mount, which is a different question.**

## Context

`repository-model.md` section 7 is the only picture of the estate the
documents hold. It draws the modules under `module/{foundation, capability,
operation, platform}` and `test/playground` — forty-one submodules — and stops
there.

The manifest declares 330 repositories: 18 Capability, 10 Foundation, 9
Operation, 3 Platform and 290 Technology. **None of the 290 appears on that
tree.** A reader looking for `xmip-core-transport-mllp` finds it in no
document, only by reading 1,170 lines of TOML.

Being hand-drawn is also why the tree was wrong. On 2026-09-19 it still said
eighteen capability entries where there were nineteen, and it still carried
`migrate`, `diagnose` and `schedule` after ADR-0058's amendment unmounted
them. Section 7 already carries the confession in its own margin: *the drawing
had rotted exactly as clause 5 of ADR-0020 predicts a drawing will*.

ADR-0020 clause 5 is the rule that seems to forbid the fix: *Any document that
lists repositories is a second source of truth and will be wrong within a
week. Documents hold reasoning; the manifest holds facts.* It retired three
hand-written repository inventories. It was right about all three, and it is
the reason this record exists rather than a fourth inventory.

The distinction clause 5 did not have to draw in August is between a **copy**
and a **view**. A copy drifts because nothing holds it to its source. A view
cannot drift, because regenerating it is how it is written and a test fails
when the committed file is not what the generator produces. ADR-0020's own
index is already such a view: `doc/decision/README.md` is generated by
`New-XmipDecisionIndex` and held by `test/Decision.Test.ps1`, in the document
that indexes ADR-0020.

One fact the manifest genuinely does not hold: what is composed here.
Composition lives in the `.gitmodules` files, at two levels since ADR-0016's
2026-09-07 amendment — the root composes the modules, and a technology is
composed by its own parent capability, inside that repository. No document
said how many of the 330 are on disk, and nobody could answer it without
walking twelve `.gitmodules` files by hand.

## Decision

1. **The estate map is a generated document.** `New-XmipEstateMap` writes
   `doc/architecture/estate-map.md`. It emits the document to the pipeline and
   `-Save` writes it, the shape `New-XmipDecisionIndex` already has, so the
   two generators are read the same way. `Get-XmipEstateRepository` returns
   the same facts as objects, so a question can be asked without parsing a
   document.

2. **It is a view, not a copy, and the test is what makes that true.**
   `test/EstateMap.Test.ps1` regenerates the map and fails when the committed
   file differs, exactly as `test/Decision.Test.ps1` holds the decision index.
   A hand edit to the map fails the gate. This is the clause that keeps
   ADR-0020 clause 5 intact: the estate still has one source of truth, and the
   map is a rendering of it.

3. **It states what the manifest cannot: what is composed.** Mounted means
   composed as a submodule, read from the root `.gitmodules` for the modules
   and from each module's own for its technologies. A technology reported as
   not mounted is one its parent does not compose — not one the root forgot —
   and the map says so in its own words, because a reader who does not know
   composition has two levels reads a hundred technologies as missing.

4. **Maturity is reported as declared, never as observed, and the map says
   so.** The manifest's `maturity` is known to disagree with what a repository
   contains; `xmip-core-transport` is `planned` and holds six figures of Rust.
   The map makes the disagreement visible and takes no view on it. Correcting
   `architecture.toml` is the owner's decision and is not this record's.

5. **Section 7 keeps its tree.** It answers *where does a module mount*, which
   is the owner's layout and is worth drawing by hand at two levels. It gains
   a paragraph naming the map for the rest. Two documents, two questions, one
   link between them — clause 1 of ADR-0020 is not breached, because *where
   things mount* and *what the estate consists of* are not one subject.

6. **`doc/architecture/` gains a sixth document, and this is its reason.**
   ADR-0020 clause 4 requires one that is not *this document got long*. The
   reason is that this is not prose: it is a rendering of the manifest, and it
   belongs beside the document whose subject it completes. It is named for
   what it is rather than `*-model.md`, because it models nothing — the five
   models describe a system, and this describes an inventory.

## Consequences

- **The gap is on the record for the first time.** 330 declared, 253 composed
  here, 77 not. Seventy-six of the seventy-seven are technologies under seven
  capabilities that compose none — `prepare`, `process`, `transform`,
  `retain`, `audit`, `observe` and `report` — and the seventy-seventh is
  `xmip-test`, a provider namespace with nothing to mount. Twelve capabilities
  compose every technology they declare; the split is clean, and no document
  had ever stated it.
- **The maturity column is uncomfortable reading, and that is the point.** 156
  of 330 are `reserved`, the default for a repository that declares nothing,
  and 79 of those are composed and hold code. Six are `planned`. The manifest
  is behind the estate, in one direction only.
- **The map depends on a composed clone.** Reading what a capability composes
  means reading that capability's repository, so a clone whose submodules are
  not initialized generates a map reporting every technology as absent, and
  the test fails. The test says so in its own failure message. The estate's
  working assumption is a composed tree (`Get-XmipStatus`, `Publish-XmipChange`
  and the Rust style gate all make it); a map that reported the root's view
  only would be deterministic and would be wrong by two hundred and fourteen
  repositories, which is the worse of the two.
- `README.md` lists the map, which `test/Documentation.Test.ps1` requires of
  every document under `doc/architecture/`.
- **Not decided here.** Whether the map should also report observed size or
  language, which would make the maturity disagreement quantitative rather
  than visible; whether `Sync-XmipEstate -Compose` should reach depth three,
  which is what would close the gap the map now shows; and whether the seven
  uncomposed capabilities are waiting on work or on a decision.

## Alternatives considered

**Extend the section 7 tree by hand.** It is what the instruction literally
asked for, and it is the thing ADR-0020 clause 5 retired three times already.
Three hundred and thirty names in a fenced block, correct on the day and wrong
by the next landing. The tree was wrong on the day this was written, which is
the whole argument.

**Put the map in `architecture.toml` as comments.** The manifest is read by
machines and is already 1,170 lines. A reader wanting to see the estate would
be reading the source of the thing rather than a rendering of it, and TOML
comments are held by nothing.

**Generate it into `.ai-work/` or leave it to `Sync-XmipEstate -Report`.**
A report is what the estate is *now* on this machine, produced on demand and
untracked. A document in `doc/` is what the estate *is*, committed, reviewed
in a diff, and readable on GitHub without a clone. Landing a change that adds
sixty technologies should show sixty lines in the diff, which only the tracked
form gives.

**One row per repository.** 330 rows is a document nobody scrolls. The
technologies are drawn several names to a line under the repository that hosts
them, grouped by maturity, which is the shape section 7's tree already uses
for the modules and the reason it is legible.

**Mark mounted and unmounted technologies individually.** Where a parent
composes all of its technologies or none of them — which is every parent today
— a mark on eighty-four names says what one sentence says. The map marks only
the partial case, where the mark is the information.

## Provenance

The instruction is the owner's, 2026-09-19: *Xmip Estate Map is not built
out, do that.* That it must be generated rather than drawn, the reading of
ADR-0020 clause 5 as permitting a view, the two-level composition clause, the
rendering, and the consequences are the assistant's drafting. The record is
Proposed rather than Accepted until the owner says so.

Clause 4 records a refusal as much as a decision: the map reports the
manifest's maturity unchanged, including the six repositories whose
declaration is plainly behind their contents. Re-classifying them is the
owner's call and was deliberately not taken here.
